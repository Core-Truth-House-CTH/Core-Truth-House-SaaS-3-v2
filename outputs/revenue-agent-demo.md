## The Unique Mechanism

**The Revenue Agent Chain** — a strategy sequence with agent labor attached, not "an agent team":

1. **Foundation Agent** — extracts the real problem and buyer's current state from the raw idea
2. **Positioning Agent** — takes Foundation's output; no positioning before foundation is locked
3. **Offer Agent** — takes the positioning statement as hard input; no offer before positioning exists
4. **Content Angle Agent** — reverse-engineers angles from the locked offer, never from a blank idea
5. **Lead Magnet + Follow-Up Agent** — builds the capture and nurture path that routes back to the offer

Believability comes from showing the handoff on screen: the positioning line literally pasted into the offer agent's prompt. The audience sees a causal chain, not five parallel chat tabs. The sequencing IS the Core Truth House methodology — the video is a live demonstration of the platform's own architecture.

Thesis line for the video: **"The tool changes every six months. The sequence doesn't."**

## What the Creator Must Show On Screen to Make the Video Credible

1. **Unedited, timestamped screen recording** of the chain running on a real idea — no cutaways during reasoning steps, only during setup
2. **The copy-paste handoff, zoomed** — Foundation output pasted into the Positioning agent at ~4:05 is the single most important visual beat; repeat shorter zooms at every subsequent handoff (5:50, 7:35, 9:35) so the pattern builds the thesis without re-explaining

4. **The side-by-side** — a raw one-shot "write me a positioning statement" prompt vs. the sequenced chain's output on the same idea; this makes "it's the sequencing, not the tool" provable instead of asserted
5. **Timestamps stamped in the replay montage** — reinforcing "one sitting, unedited"

## What to Remove Because It Feels Generic

- **Any agent setup tutorial segment** — terminal config, subagent files, orchestration mechanics beyond a 10-15 second visual; this is the trap that collapses the video into the saturated tutorial genre
- **Prompt engineering tips of any kind** — the brief forbids it and the audience has been burned by it
- **"AI will save you time" framing** — banned; every claim must connect to attention, leads, revenue, or leverage
- **The agent roster as the star** — lead with the causal chain, not "meet my five agents"
- **Polished, templated-looking outputs** — they read as cherry-picked; the messy draft is the proof
- **"Full $20K strategist replacement" overclaiming** — frame as compressing first-draft strategy work with the founder making final calls; hype breaks the credibility the whole funnel rests on
- **Double CTAs** — one next step (template); the workshop is named as optional help, never pitched as a second ask
- **Generic listicle content angles** — every angle shown must be reverse-engineered from the locked offer on screen
